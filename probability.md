## Principles of My Statistical Practice

by Christopher Tong

UNDER CONSTRUCTION!!!

### Probabilistic thinking is underrated by those who have not been trained in its use.

Gigerenzer (2002) has provided ample documentation.

### Probabilistic thinking is overrated by those who have been so trained.

See Taleb (2010), Crane (2020), and Kay and King (2020).  You can get a flavor of the arguments they make from the first few quotes listed on my other [site](https://hydrodynamicstability.github.io/Perspectives-on-Applied-Statistics/).

### References

H. Crane, 2020:  [Naive probablism](https://researchers.one/articles/20.03.00003).  Researchers.One.

G. Gigerenzer, 2002:  *Calculated Risks:  How to Know When Numbers Deceive You*.  Simon & Schuster.

J. Kay and M. King, 2020:  *Radical Uncertainty: Decision-Making Beyond the Numbers*.  W. W. Norton.

N. N. Taleb, 2010:  *The Black Swan: The Impact of the Highly Improbable*, second edition.  Random House.
